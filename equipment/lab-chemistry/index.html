How it works

Automated clinical chemistry analyzer

An automated photometer: it pipettes samples and reagents into cuvettes, incubates them at a controlled temperature, and measures colour change or ion potentials to calculate analyte concentrations.

Principle

Most routine chemistry tests are photometric: a sample is mixed with reagents that produce a coloured product or change in absorbance in proportion to the analyte (end-point methods) or to the rate of an enzymatic reaction (kinetic methods). The analyser automates this: a sample probe and reagent probes aspirate and dispense microlitre volumes into cuvettes on a reaction disk held at a stable temperature (commonly 37 °C), mixers stir the reaction, and a photometer measures absorbance at chosen wavelengths through each cuvette over time. Turbidimetric methods measure light lost to scattering (immunoassays for specific proteins). Electrolytes such as sodium, potassium and chloride are usually measured by ion-selective electrodes (ISE), which develop a potential related to ion activity. Results are calculated against calibrators and checked with quality-control samples. Analytical quality depends as much on water, temperature, pipetting precision and clean cuvettes as on the optics, so most service work is fluidics and cleanliness.

Site and infrastructure

  • Purified water system meeting the manufacturer's water quality specification (resistivity/conductivity, microbiological limits) and supply flow; water quality monitored.
  • Drain for liquid waste or approved waste containers, compliant with local biohazard and chemical waste rules.
  • Stable mains supply with UPS of adequate capacity; protective earth.
  • Room temperature and humidity within manufacturer limits and stable; no direct sunlight or air conditioning blowing on the analyser.
  • Level, vibration-free bench or floor with service clearance around the analyser.
  • Network connection to the LIS.

Preventive maintenance basics

  • Inspect and clean sample and reagent probes; check alignment and level detection; replace worn probes.
  • Replace syringe seals, valve parts and tubing per schedule; check for leaks and bubbles.
  • Clean and check cuvettes, run cuvette blank and photometer checks; replace lamp per hours or energy check.
  • Verify incubation temperature with a calibrated thermometer.
  • Clean wash station nozzles and check detergent and water supply.
  • Maintain ISE: replace electrodes and reference as needed, check slopes.
  • Verify water quality and clean water tank, filters and lines; check waste lines.
  • After maintenance run calibration and QC to confirm performance; document.

Safety

  • Treat all samples and the analyser's wetted parts and waste as potentially infectious; wear gloves, gown and eye protection and decontaminate before service.
  • Reagents and detergents can be corrosive or toxic; follow safety data sheets.
  • Moving arms and probes can cause puncture injuries; stop the analyser or enter maintenance mode before reaching into the work area.
  • Lamps run hot; allow to cool before replacement and do not touch the glass.
  • Dispose of waste and replaced parts as biohazardous according to local regulations.
