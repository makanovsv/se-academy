How it works

Diagnostic ultrasound imaging

A probe of piezoelectric elements sends short acoustic pulses into tissue and listens for echoes; the system beamforms them into real-time images and Doppler flow data.

Principle

The transducer contains an array of piezoelectric elements (or, in some designs, single crystal or capacitive micromachined elements) that convert electrical pulses into ultrasound, typically in the low-megahertz range for deep imaging and higher frequencies for superficial work, and convert returning echoes back into voltages. Because the system assumes a fixed average speed of sound in tissue (about 1540 m/s), the time of arrival of each echo gives its depth. A beamformer applies precise delays to the elements on transmit and receive to steer and focus the beam; after amplification, time-gain compensation, digitisation and envelope detection, each scan line becomes a column of brightness values (B-mode). Doppler modes measure the frequency or phase shift of echoes from moving blood to display velocity (colour, pulsed and continuous-wave Doppler). Most of the electronics is low voltage, but transmit pulses reach high voltage inside the system and probe. For the field engineer, the probe is by far the most frequent point of failure.

Site and infrastructure

  • Grounded mains outlet with correct voltage; in areas with unstable supply, power conditioning or UPS recommended by the manufacturer.
  • Room temperature and humidity within manufacturer limits; adequate airflow around the system vents.
  • Network connection for PACS and worklist where used.
  • Safe, tidy routing of probe cables and probe holders to prevent cable crushing and drops.
  • Approved disinfectants and probe reprocessing area (including high-level disinfection for endocavity and TEE probes).

Preventive maintenance basics

  • Inspect every probe: lens for cracks, swelling or delamination, housing, cable and strain relief, connector pins.
  • Check each probe for dead elements using an image of air or a phantom, or a dedicated probe tester; keep a record per serial number.
  • Perform electrical safety tests per IEC 62353 (or the locally required standard), including patient-leakage on applied parts; TEE probes need extra attention.
  • Clean air filters and check fans; review temperature and error logs.
  • Check image quality with a tissue-mimicking phantom: penetration, resolution, uniformity, compared with baseline.
  • Check trackball, controls, touch screen, display, and printers.
  • Verify disk space, backup of presets, DICOM and network configuration.

Safety

  • Damaged probe lens or housing can expose the patient to electrical leakage; take such probes out of service and tag them.
  • Internal transmit circuits carry high voltage; disconnect mains and allow discharge before opening the system.
  • Treat all probes as potentially contaminated; clean and disinfect according to the reprocessing instructions before handling, especially endocavity and TEE probes.
  • Observe ESD precautions when handling boards and probe connectors.
  • Battery-powered portable systems: handle lithium batteries per manufacturer guidance; swollen batteries are a fire risk.
