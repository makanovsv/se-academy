How it works

Intensive care mechanical ventilator

A closed-loop gas delivery system that mixes air and oxygen, delivers controlled breaths through valves and sensors, and protects the patient with alarms and a safety valve.

Course: Ventilator Service — Level 1
01

Principle

An ICU ventilator takes high-pressure medical air and oxygen from the hospital pipeline or cylinders (or uses an internal turbine/blower for air), reduces and regulates them, mixes them to the set oxygen concentration, and delivers each breath through a proportional inspiratory valve. A controller closes the loop on flow and pressure sensors to deliver either a target volume or a target pressure, according to the mode, and synchronises with the patient's own effort using pressure or flow triggers. During expiration an actively controlled expiratory valve holds the set PEEP. The delivered gas is monitored independently (pressure, flow/volume, FiO2), and alarms warn of disconnection, obstruction, high pressure, low minute volume, apnoea, gas supply failure and technical faults. A mechanical safety valve opens if pressure becomes excessive, and an ambient (anti-asphyxia) valve lets the patient breathe room air if the ventilator fails. Gas is humidified by a heated humidifier or an HME filter. The ventilator is life-support equipment: every repair must end with a full performance verification.

04

Safety

Site and infrastructure

  • Medical gas pipeline outlets for O2 and medical air at the pressure range specified by the ventilator manufacturer, with correct non-interchangeable connectors; check for water and oil in medical air.
  • Backup gas: cylinders with regulators available for transport and pipeline failure.
  • Grounded mains outlets on essential (emergency-generator-backed) circuits; ventilator internal battery charged.
  • Exhaust and room ventilation adequate, especially where oxygen concentrations may build up near bedspaces.
  • Room temperature and humidity within operating range; no use near MRI unless the device is labelled MR-conditional for that environment.

Preventive maintenance basics

  • Follow the manufacturer's preventive maintenance kit schedule (seals, diaphragms, filters, O2 cell, batteries) based on operating hours or time.
  • Calibrate flow, pressure and O2 sensors; run the full self-test with a test lung.
  • Verify performance with a calibrated gas flow analyser: tidal volume, pressure, PEEP, FiO2, flow, in several modes, against tolerance.
  • Test all alarms: disconnection, high pressure, low minute volume, apnoea, gas supply loss, power failure, O2 limits.
  • Check safety valve and ambient (anti-asphyxia) valve function.
  • Test battery run time and switch-over from mains to battery.
  • Perform electrical safety testing (IEC 62353 or locally required standard).
  • Inspect and clean inlet filters, water traps, fan filters; inspect gas connectors and hoses for wear and leaks.

Safety

  • Never service a ventilator connected to a patient; arrange a replacement and transfer before any work.
  • Oxygen enriches combustion: no oil or grease on oxygen fittings, no open flames or sparks, and use only oxygen-compatible lubricants where the manufacturer specifies them.
  • Compressed gases: depressurise and disconnect supplies before opening the pneumatic system; cylinders must be secured and handled with care.
  • Treat expiratory components, filters and sensors as contaminated; use PPE and follow reprocessing instructions.
  • After any repair, the device must pass full performance verification and alarm checks before return to clinical use; document the result.
