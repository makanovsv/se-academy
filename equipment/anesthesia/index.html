How it works

Anaesthesia delivery workstation

A gas delivery system, vaporiser and rebreathing circle with a ventilator, built so that the patient always receives oxygen and the anaesthetist always knows what is being delivered.

Principle

The workstation receives oxygen, nitrous oxide (where used) and medical air from pipeline or back-up cylinders, reduces them to stable pressures and meters them into a fresh gas flow, either with mechanical flowmeters or electronic flow control. Safety devices such as an oxygen failure protection device and a hypoxic guard (mechanical or electronic link between O2 and N2O) prevent delivery of a hypoxic mixture. The fresh gas passes through a vaporiser that adds a controlled concentration of volatile agent, then enters a circle breathing system: exhaled gas passes through a CO2 absorber and is rebreathed, which saves agent and conserves heat and moisture, while an adjustable pressure-limiting (APL) valve and the scavenging system remove excess gas. A built-in ventilator (bellows, piston or turbine-driven) replaces the reservoir bag in mechanical mode. Monitoring of inspired O2, agent, CO2, airway pressure and volumes, plus a leak and compliance check before each case, closes the loop. Because the device sits between high-pressure gas and an unconscious patient, leak tightness and correct gas identity matter more than any electronic feature.

Site and infrastructure

  • Medical gas outlets (O2, air, N2O where used) at the pressures specified by the manufacturer, correctly labelled and with non-interchangeable connectors.
  • Full back-up O2 cylinder on the machine (or immediately available) with a working regulator.
  • Active anaesthetic gas scavenging system connected and working at the specified flow.
  • Mains outlets on essential (generator-backed) supply; workstation battery charged.
  • Theatre ventilation and temperature/humidity within design and manufacturer limits; antistatic and fire safety measures in place.

Preventive maintenance basics

  • Replace seals, O-rings, flow sensors, O2 cells and other parts in the manufacturer's PM kit at the specified interval.
  • Perform high- and low-pressure leak tests and circle system leak/compliance tests to specification.
  • Test O2 failure protection device, alarms and hypoxic guard behaviour.
  • Verify flow accuracy, ventilator volume and pressure delivery and PEEP with a calibrated analyser.
  • Check vaporiser output with a calibrated agent analyser (or arrange vaporiser service as required) and check interlocks and mounts.
  • Calibrate and verify the gas monitor (O2, CO2, agent) and check water traps and sample lines.
  • Test battery autonomy and switch-over; perform electrical safety testing.
  • Check scavenging interface operation and pressure relief.

Safety

  • Never service a workstation in use; after any repair, perform the full pre-use check and performance verification before clinical use.
  • Oxygen and nitrous oxide both support combustion; no oil or grease on fittings, no ignition sources, use only lubricants approved by the manufacturer.
  • Depressurise pipeline and cylinder supplies before opening the pneumatic system; open cylinder valves slowly.
  • Exposure to waste anaesthetic agents: work with scavenging connected and the room ventilated; avoid spilling liquid agent and handle vaporiser filling with care.
  • Gas identity: never alter, adapt or bypass gas-specific connectors; a crossed gas connection can be fatal.
