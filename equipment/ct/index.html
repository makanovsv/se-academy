How it works

Multislice computed tomography

An X-ray tube and a curved multi-row detector rotating around the patient on a slip-ring gantry, measuring attenuation from hundreds of angles and reconstructing cross-sectional images.

Principle

A rotating-anode X-ray tube, fed by a high-voltage generator (typically 80-140 kV), produces a fan or cone beam that is shaped by filters, a bowtie filter and a collimator. After passing through the patient, the remaining radiation hits a curved array of detector elements, usually ceramic scintillators coupled to photodiodes, arranged in many rows. A data acquisition system (DAS) on the rotor digitises every channel for each angular position. Tube, generator, detector and DAS all sit on a rotor that turns continuously; power reaches it through slip rings and data leaves it through a contactless link. While the table moves through the gantry (helical scan), the reconstructor uses the projections and the exact table position to compute attenuation values, expressed in Hounsfield units, for every voxel. A practical rule for fault finding: problems 'before the patient' (tube, generator, filtration) change dose, noise and brightness of the whole image; problems 'after the patient' (detector, DAS, data link) produce localised, geometrically regular defects such as rings and streaks.

Symptom → where to look first

SymptomSuspect first
Concentric ring on imagesDetector channel: run air calibration first; a persistent ring after calibration points to a detector module or its electronics. Ring radius indicates the channel.
Image noise rising at unchanged protocolTube: anode wear reduces output; check tube counters and arc log, then generator kV/mA accuracy.
Frequent 'cooling' waits between patientsRoom climate and tube cooling first (air conditioning, filters, heat exchanger, oil circuit), then tube heat-storage behaviour near end of life.
Scan aborts with arc / spit errorsTube vacuum degradation or HV cable/connector breakdown; trend error frequency, inspect HV connections.
Sharp streaks or missing projectionsDAS and data link: dirt on contactless transmitter/receiver, link errors; slip-ring communication if errors coincide with rotation.
Intermittent errors from many unrelated subsystemsPower and slip rings: supply dips, earthing, worn brushes and carbon dust.
Wrong slice spacing or geometry mismatchTable: encoder and drive accuracy, measured travel vs commanded.
Growing hum or vibration during rotationRotor bearing or unbalance (especially after tube change); trend the noise and plan replacement early.
Scanner works but images do not reach PACS / system freezesReconstructor and host: disk space, archive, network and DICOM settings.

Site and infrastructure

  • Electrical supply rated for peak generator demand, measured per phase under load; dedicated protective earth; emergency stop circuits functional.
  • Room air conditioning sized for the gantry and electronics heat load; temperature and humidity within manufacturer limits all year. Undersized cooling is a leading real-world cause of CT downtime.
  • Radiation shielding of the room built to the approved shielding design; doors, windows and warning lights / interlocks working; radiation protection survey on record.
  • Gantry and electronics cooling (air or water as designed): filters, heat exchanger, chiller where used.
  • Floor loading and level for gantry and table.
  • Network and storage: PACS connection, sufficient local archive, time synchronisation.

Preventive maintenance basics

  • Read and trend tube counters (scan seconds, exposures, heat units) and arc errors at every visit; forecast tube replacement before it fails.
  • Inspect slip-ring brushes and rings, clean carbon dust, check contactless data link for dirt.
  • Clean or replace gantry and electronics air filters; check heat exchanger, fans, oil leaks.
  • Run air calibration and the daily/periodic QC phantom checks: CT number of water, noise, uniformity, slice thickness, and compare with baseline.
  • Check gantry bearing sound and vibration, drive belt condition, tilt, lasers and emergency stops.
  • Verify table travel accuracy against commanded positions and check table drive, rails and weight limits.
  • Review error logs; check disk space, archive, PACS transfer.
  • Measure room temperature and humidity and supply voltage under load, and record them.

Safety

  • Ionising radiation: never be in the scan room during exposure; use the system's service modes only with the room door closed and warning light active; wear a personal dosimeter.
  • High voltage: the generator and tube operate at tens of kilovolts with stored energy; follow lockout, wait for capacitor discharge and verify zero voltage before touching HV parts.
  • Rotating mass: never work inside the gantry unless rotation is mechanically and electrically disabled; the rotor stores large kinetic energy.
  • Heavy parts: tubes, detectors and rotor components require proper lifting means and two people; rotor must be locked during component exchange and balanced afterwards.
  • Carbon dust from slip-ring brushes is conductive; clean with the manufacturer-approved method and avoid inhaling it.
