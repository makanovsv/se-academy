How it works

Multiparameter patient monitor

A set of sensor front ends (ECG, SpO2, NIBP, IBP, temperature, CO2) feeding one processor that displays trends and raises alarms, often over a hospital network.

Principle

Each parameter has its own measurement chain. ECG uses skin electrodes and a high-impedance differential amplifier with strong common-mode rejection to pick up millivolt-level cardiac potentials. Pulse oximetry (SpO2) shines red and infrared light through tissue and computes oxygen saturation from the ratio of the pulsatile absorptions. Non-invasive blood pressure (NIBP) inflates a cuff and uses the oscillometric method, analysing pressure oscillations during deflation to estimate systolic, mean and diastolic pressure. Invasive pressure (IBP) uses a disposable strain-gauge transducer in a fluid-filled line. Temperature is measured with thermistors; capnography measures CO2 by infrared absorption either mainstream at the airway or sidestream by aspirating a sample. The patient-connected front ends are electrically isolated from mains and from each other to keep leakage currents within limits and to survive defibrillation. A main processor runs algorithms, trends and alarms, and the monitor usually connects to a central station over a wired or wireless network. Most field complaints come from accessories (cables, sensors, cuffs, hoses) rather than the monitor itself.

Site and infrastructure

  • Grounded mains outlets; equipotential bonding where required in critical care and theatres.
  • Wired network ports or Wi-Fi coverage for all monitored bedspaces where a central station is used.
  • Secure mounting (wall, rail, ceiling pendant) rated for the monitor and modules.
  • Room temperature and humidity within operating range; adequate ventilation around the monitor.
  • Stock of compatible accessories (ECG cables, SpO2 sensors, cuffs, CO2 lines); incompatible accessories cause both errors and safety problems.

Preventive maintenance basics

  • Inspect all accessories: ECG cables and leads, SpO2 sensors, NIBP cuffs and hoses, temperature probes, CO2 lines; replace damaged items.
  • Verify ECG, SpO2, IBP and temperature with a patient simulator; check alarm activation and limits.
  • Perform NIBP checks: static pressure accuracy against a calibrated manometer, leak test, over-pressure protection test.
  • Verify capnography calibration and check the water trap and sampling pump.
  • Test battery capacity and mains/battery switch-over.
  • Perform electrical safety testing including patient leakage on each applied part (IEC 62353 or local standard).
  • Check network connection to the central station and time synchronisation; review error logs.

Safety

  • Patient isolation must never be compromised: do not use damaged cables or non-compatible accessories; an insulation failure on an applied part can create a leakage path to the patient.
  • Defibrillation-proof inputs depend on correct cables and modules; after repair, confirm the module and cables are the specified type.
  • Disconnect from mains before opening; some power supplies retain charge.
  • Never disable or lower alarm volume and limits as part of servicing without restoring the hospital's configured defaults.
  • Treat accessories and the monitor surface as contaminated; clean and disinfect before work.
