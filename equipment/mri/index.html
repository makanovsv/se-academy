How it works

Magnetic resonance imaging

A superconducting magnet, three gradient coils and an RF chain that turn hydrogen nuclei into a spatially encoded radio signal, then into images.

Interactive. Hover to open the magnet; stop the compressor and watch the helium level. Open full screen ↗

Principle

A superconducting coil held in liquid helium at about 4.2 K produces a strong, permanent static field (B0, typically 1.5 T or 3 T) that aligns the spins of hydrogen nuclei and makes them precess at the Larmor frequency, roughly 42.58 MHz per tesla (about 64 MHz at 1.5 T, 128 MHz at 3 T). An RF transmit pulse at that frequency tips the magnetisation; as it relaxes, it induces a weak signal in the receive coils. Three gradient coils (X, Y, Z) superimpose small, rapidly switched field slopes on B0 so that frequency and phase of the signal depend on position; the reconstructor then turns this frequency/phase-encoded data (k-space) into images with a Fourier transform. For the engineer the machine is four systems sharing one room: the magnet and its cryogenics, the gradient chain, the RF chain inside a Faraday cage, and the computing chain. The field is always on, even with the building power off.

The chain, block by block

Magnet and cryostat (B0)Superconducting coil in liquid helium inside a vacuum-insulated cryostat with thermal shields; provides the static field and, with passive and active shims, its homogeneity.Homogeneity drift (geometric distortion, poor fat suppression) often has an external cause such as new steel near the magnet. Loss of vacuum or cooling shows as rising helium boil-off and, in the worst case, a quench.

Symptom → where to look first

SymptomSuspect first
Helium level steadily fallingCryocooler first: compressor running and cooled? water flow and temperature, adsorber hours, cold-head sound and frost. Do not just top up; find why helium stopped re-condensing.
Bright line or 'zipper' across images at a fixed positionFaraday cage first: door closed and finger-stock contacts intact, any electronic device in the room, cables bypassing the filter panel. Then a noisy component inside the room.
Local signal loss or uneven brightness in one areaReceive coil: wrong or damaged element, connector or cable. Swap to another coil of the same type; if the problem moves with the coil, it is the coil.
Uniform loss of signal on all coilsTransmit chain: RF amplifier, body coil, transmit/receive switching; also check centre frequency and RF calibration (prescan) results.
Exams abort with gradient over-temperature or current errorsCooling first: chiller, water flow and temperature to the gradient coil and amplifiers, technical-room temperature. Only then the amplifier itself.
Geometric distortion or poor fat suppression appearing over timeB0 homogeneity: new ferromagnetic objects or building changes near the magnet, then shim. Also eddy-current compensation for gradient-related distortion.
New knocking, rattling or buzzing noise during scansMechanical: loose covers, panels, coil mounts or objects in the bore; a change in noise character is a symptom even though MRI is normally loud.
System scans but images do not arrive or reconstruction hangsReconstructor and host: disk space, archive, fans, network and DICOM configuration before suspecting acquisition hardware.
Cold head cycling sound changed or stoppedCryocooler: compressor fault, power, water cooling, helium circuit pressure; treat as urgent because boil-off starts as soon as cooling stops.

Site and infrastructure

  • Electrical supply: dedicated feed with correct voltage and phases measured under load, protective earth, and the site's emergency power-off; the cryocooler compressor should be on a supply that keeps running or restarts reliably, because a stopped compressor starts helium boil-off.
  • Chilled water: capacity, supply temperature, flow and pressure to the gradient/electronics cooling and the helium compressor, as specified by the manufacturer; check for leaks, filter condition and alarm function.
  • Room climate: temperature and humidity in the magnet room and technical room within manufacturer limits, year-round, including summer peaks.
  • RF shielding integrity: door contacts, window, filter panel and waveguides intact; no unauthorised penetrations.
  • Quench (vent) pipe: continuous path to the outside, supports intact, outlet unobstructed and protected, discharge area away from people and air intakes.
  • Oxygen monitor in the magnet room working and within its check interval; room ventilation and emergency exhaust functioning.
  • Magnetic field zoning: controlled-access zones and the 0.5 mT (5 gauss) line marked; no new ferromagnetic structures or moving steel (lifts, vehicles, cabinets) close to the magnet.
  • Floor and vibration: floor loading rated for the magnet; no new sources of vibration nearby.

Preventive maintenance basics

  • Record helium level, cryostat pressure, compressor pressures, cold-head and adsorber hours, technical-room temperature at every visit and keep a trend; a single reading says little, the slope says everything.
  • Inspect the cold head and lines: listen to the cycling sound, look for frost or condensation, check compressor water flow and temperatures.
  • Check gradient and electronics cooling: flow, pressure, temperature, filters, leaks, chiller alarms.
  • Test every receive coil: recognised by the system, run a test scan per coil, inspect connectors, latches, cables and housings.
  • Inspect the RF door contacts and shield penetrations; check for zipper artefacts on a test scan.
  • Run the manufacturer's periodic image-quality phantom test (SNR, uniformity, geometry, ghosting) and compare with baseline.
  • Review error and magnet logs; check disk space, archive and PACS transfer.
  • Verify safety items: oxygen monitor, quench pipe condition, 0.5 mT line marking, emergency buttons accessible and guarded, signage.

Safety

  • The magnetic field is always on. Before entering Zone IV remove every ferromagnetic object from your pockets and body, every time; bring in only tools and test equipment verified as MR-safe or MR-conditional.
  • Any ferromagnetic object (oxygen cylinder, ladder, trolley, tool) becomes a projectile near the bore. Never let unchecked equipment through the door, including from contractors.
  • Persons with pacemakers, neurostimulators or other active implants must not cross the 0.5 mT line without screening; the engineer is responsible for anyone they bring.
  • Never operate the magnet emergency rundown (quench) button except to free a trapped person in a life-threatening situation; never open cryostat valves, vacuum ports or disconnect pressurised helium lines without the manufacturer's procedure and training.
  • In a quench (loud venting, white cloud, falling oxygen) evacuate everyone immediately and do not re-enter until oxygen level is confirmed normal; cold helium gas displaces oxygen and can cause asphyxiation and cold burns. The door may be hard to open due to room over-pressure.
  • Gradient and RF amplifiers carry hazardous voltages and stored energy; apply lockout and wait for discharge before opening cabinets.
