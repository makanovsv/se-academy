Mindray

SV600 / SV650

Mindray's mid-to-high-end ICU ventilator for adult, paediatric and (with the neonatal module) neonatal patients, running on wall air and O2 or on an optional internal blower, used in ICUs, emergency departments and for in-hospital transport.

Architecture

Gas inlets, regulators and gas switching

Air and O2 enter through inlets with a water-trap filter and regulators. On units with a blower, a gas-switch three-way valve chooses between wall air and the blower. Typical failures: a cracked water-trap bowl or broken drain pin, regulator output drift from an aged spring, and supply-pressure alarms caused by the wall supply rather than the machine. The service A/D-channel screen shows the measured supply pressures; compare them with an external gauge.

Inspiratory limbs: proportional valves and TSI flow sensors

Each gas has its own proportional valve and a thermal (TSI) flow sensor, mounted on a proportional-valve manifold. Valve calibration fails when the valve leaks closed, opens at too high a minimum flow, or gives non-monotonic flow against current. Air and O2 sensor types must match their limbs. Any valve or sensor replacement needs a factory flow calibration.

Blower (turbine) module (option)

A blower box with its own battery, fan, HEPA filter, large-diameter inspiratory valve and pilot valve. It lets the unit ventilate without wall air. It fails with over-temperature (blocked vents, hot room), HEPA missing or due for replacement, blower failure, and a leaking or stuck three-way valve.

Control and protection electronics

The main control board in the display runs the user interface. The ventilation control module (VCM) does the ventilation control and holds pressure sensors and the buzzer. A separate protection module (VPM) cross-checks it, and a power board with DC-DC supplies handles the batteries. Communication-stop and self-test errors point to cables, the module itself, or software versions that do not match between boards.

Pressure sensing and safety valve

Inspiratory and expiratory pressure sensors are zeroed through three-way valves, and an atmospheric pressure sensor sits on both the VCM and the VPM. The safety valve (diaphragm, seals and an electromagnet) opens the circuit to air when it must. Its diaphragm and seals fail with age, and a diaphragm that does not reset properly causes leaks.

Expiratory valve and expiratory flow sensor

A voice-coil-driven expiratory valve with a replaceable membrane, plus the expiratory flow sensor. This is the main source of PEEP, leak and expiratory-volume problems. The membrane ages or tears, the valve is reassembled wrongly after cleaning, or the flow sensor drifts and needs zeroing and calibration.

O2 monitoring

Either a galvanic O2 cell (a consumable that gets used up) or a paramagnetic O2 sensor option. Both need 21% and 100% calibration. Alarms tell you whether the sensor is disconnected, exhausted or not calibrated.

Power, batteries and plug-in parameters

Two host batteries, plus a blower battery on blower units, are managed by the DC-DC power board, with temperature and communication monitoring for each battery. Optional neonatal flow module, sidestream or mainstream CO2, SpO2, nebuliser and heater each have their own fault messages.

Faults and how to find them

Supply pressure alarms (air or O2 supply low, both low, or 'no gas supply can be used'), sometimes on several units at once, even after PM with new filters, O2 cell and battery (field case on the sister SV300).field case
Likely causes
  • Low or unstable wall O2 / air pressure at the outlet
  • Supply hose or inlet fitting restriction
  • Cracked water-trap filter bowl or damaged drain pin causing a leak
  • Drifting supply pressure sensor
Checks, in order
  1. Watch how the machine is connected and how the self-test is run before you blame it
  2. Measure outlet pressure with an external gauge while gas is flowing
  3. On the service A/D channel screen, compare the measured air and O2 supply pressures with the gauge
  4. Inspect the inlet water-trap filter bowl and drain pin
Fix

Correct the gas supply or hoses. Replace the water-trap filter bowl or the drifting supply pressure sensor or board if its reading disagrees with the gauge.

O2 sensor alarms: 'O2 sensor not connected', 'O2 sensor used up', 'O2 sensor not calibrated', or O2 calibration fails with an AD value out of range at 21% or 100%.reference
Likely causes
  • Exhausted galvanic O2 cell
  • Cell cable disconnected
  • Calibration not done after the cell was replaced
  • Paramagnetic sensor needs its own calibration
Checks, in order
  1. Reconnect and inspect the cell cable
  2. Check the cell age
  3. Run the 21% and 100% O2 calibration, and follow the on-screen prompts on time (paramagnetic calibration times out if you are slow)
Fix

Replace the O2 cell and calibrate. If the cell's 21% reading is below the lower limit, the cell is finished.

'Please check exp. flow sensor', expiratory flow sensor zero point error, or expiratory flow calibration errors (wrong resolution or range).reference
Likely causes
  • Wet or dirty expiratory flow sensor
  • Sensor at end of life
  • Sensor not zeroed or calibrated after cleaning or replacement
Checks, in order
  1. Clean and dry the expiratory valve assembly and flow sensor
  2. Perform pressure and flow zeroing from the factory service menu
  3. Run the factory flow calibration of the expiratory flow sensor
Fix

Replace the expiratory flow sensor or the expiratory valve assembly if zeroing and calibration fail, then recalibrate.

PEEP high or PEEP low alarms, or PEEP not held.reference
Likely causes
  • Expiratory valve not installed correctly
  • Torn or aged expiratory valve membrane
  • Pressure sensor drift
  • Leak in the patient circuit
Checks, in order
  1. Reinstall the expiratory valve and check the membrane
  2. Check pressure sensor accuracy against a calibrated analyser (service menu accuracy check)
  3. Check the circuit for leaks
  4. Review the settings
Fix

Replace the membrane or the expiratory valve, or recalibrate the pressure sensors if they are inaccurate.

'TV not achieved', 'Ppeak not reached', leak or disconnect alarms during use.reference
Likely causes
  • Leaking or disconnected patient circuit
  • Inaccurate flow or pressure sensor
  • Settings that ask for more than the circuit or patient allows
Checks, in order
  1. Check the circuit connections and replace a leaking circuit
  2. Check flow and pressure sensor accuracy with an analyser
  3. Recalibrate if they are out of tolerance
  4. Review the settings
Fix

Replace the circuit or the leaking part, and recalibrate the sensors that are out of tolerance.

'Air insp. limb failure' or 'O2 limb failure', or the proportional-valve calibration fails (non-monotonic, or cannot reach high flow within the time limit).reference
Likely causes
  • Supply pressure too low for the calibration
  • Faulty proportional valve (leaks closed, unstable, or too high a minimum flow)
  • Damaged air or O2 TSI flow sensor
Checks, in order
  1. Confirm the supply pressure of that gas first
  2. On the Valve Test screen, drive the valve with a fixed current and check the resulting flow
  3. Check the TSI flow readings for plausibility
Fix

Replace the proportional valve or the TSI flow sensor and run the factory flow calibration.

Expiratory valve calibration fails (great leakage, cannot seal, pressure does not rise with valve current).reference
Likely causes
  • Expiratory valve not seated
  • Damaged membrane or valve port
  • Leak in the calibration tubing
  • Calibration tube too long or too compliant
Checks, in order
  1. Reinstall the expiratory valve
  2. Check the tubing for leaks
  3. Use a shorter, stiffer calibration tube
  4. Zero the calibration device before starting
Fix

Replace the expiratory valve or membrane if the calibration still fails with good tubing.

Pressure sensor failure or pressure sensor zero point error (zero AD out of range, or reading does not rise with pressure).reference
Likely causes
  • Blocked sampling line or pressure sensor filter
  • Faulty zeroing three-way valve
  • Failed pressure sensor or the board carrying it
Checks, in order
  1. On the Data Monitor screen, read the zero values of each sensor
  2. Check the sampling line and filter
  3. On the Valve Test screen, check that the zeroing three-way valves switch
  4. Run the factory zeroing
Fix

Replace the three-way valve, the sensor, or the board or VCM that carries the faulty sensor, then calibrate the pressure sensors.

'Safety valve failure' alarm.reference
Likely causes
  • Damaged or wrongly installed safety valve diaphragm
  • Lost or damaged sealing rings on the valve body, plug or floating mechanism
  • Faulty electromagnet, or diaphragm not resetting
Checks, in order
  1. Remove and inspect the safety valve diaphragm and all of its seals
  2. Check that the electromagnet operates
Fix

Replace the diaphragm, seals or the safety valve assembly, then run the system self-test and a performance check.

Blower alarms: blower temperature high, blower failure, blower technical error, 'HEPA filter missing', 'Replace HEPA filter', or gas-switch three-way valve failure.reference
Likely causes
  • Room too hot, or the fan intake or exhaust is blocked
  • Failed blower fan or blower
  • HEPA filter missing or due for replacement
  • Faulty three-way gas-switch valve or its microswitch
Checks, in order
  1. Check the room temperature against the machine's operating limit
  2. Clear the vents
  3. Check the internal and external blower temperatures on the service screen
  4. Check the HEPA filter
Fix

Replace the HEPA filter, fan, gas-switch valve or blower box as indicated, then calibrate the large-diameter inspiratory valve and flows.

Battery alarms: battery 1/2 failure, battery temperature error (unable to charge), battery communication error, low battery, or running on battery with mains connected.reference
Likely causes
  • Aged battery
  • Hot environment
  • Mains input fault (cord, socket, fuse)
  • DC-DC board not charging
Checks, in order
  1. Check the mains cord, socket and fuse
  2. Check the 12 V rail on the power board data monitor
  3. Do one full conditioning cycle (full charge, then full discharge)
  4. Check the battery cables
Fix

Replace the failed battery, or the DC-DC board if batteries will not charge.

Communication or self-test errors: control module communication stop, protection module communication stop, power board communication stop, module communication error after a board swap.reference
Likely causes
  • Loose board-to-board cable
  • Software version on the replaced board not compatible with the main board
  • Failed VCM, VPM, power board or main board
Checks, in order
  1. Reseat or replace the communication cables
  2. Compare the software versions of all modules
  3. Restart and check whether the error repeats
Fix

Load matching software on all boards, then replace the module that keeps failing.

Atmospheric pressure sensor failure.reference
Likely causes
  • One of the two atmospheric sensors (VCM or VPM) has drifted
Checks, in order
  1. On the A/D channel screen, compare the VCM and VPM readings with the real local atmospheric pressure
Fix

Replace the sensor or module whose reading is further from the true value.

Neonatal flow sensor alarms: 'No Neo. Flow Sensor', reversed sensor, sensor overrange, sensor failure, wrong sensor type.reference
Likely causes
  • Sensor connected backwards
  • Contaminated sensor
  • Adult sensor used in neonatal mode
  • Module cable or tubing fault
Checks, in order
  1. Check the sensor type and direction
  2. Check the neonatal module cables and tubes
  3. Check that neonatal monitoring is switched on in setup
Fix

Clean or replace the neonatal flow sensor.

Field notes

On the sister SV300, three units showed errors that persisted through a full PM (new filters, O2 cell and battery). The log pointed to low O2 inlet pressure. Before you condemn a Mindray ventilator, check how it is connected to the wall and how the self-test is being run.
