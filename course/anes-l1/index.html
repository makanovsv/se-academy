Anaesthesia Workstation Service — Level 1

Gas circuit, leak tests and preventive maintenance

An anaesthesia workstation is a gas system, a ventilator, a monitor and a set of safety interlocks in one cart. This course teaches the engineer to follow the gas path, run leak and checkout tests properly, maintain the machine and verify it before it goes back to theatre.

01

A leak you did not find is anaesthetic in the theatre and a patient who wakes up.

  • An anaesthesia workstation sits between high-pressure gas and an unconscious patient: a leak, a wrong mixture or a failed oxygen protection reaches the patient and the theatre air directly.
  • Machines pass their morning self-test while a sensor has drifted, a vaporiser delivers the wrong concentration or the scavenging pulls gas from the circuit - and nobody checks with an independent instrument.
  • Manufacturer courses are expensive and model-specific; the gas path, leak tests and verification that every workstation shares are rarely taught on their own.
02

After the course you can

01Understand what happens to the patient under anaesthesia and why the workstation must guarantee oxygen, so that a clinician's complaint becomes a technical question.
02Follow the whole gas path from wall outlet and cylinder through regulators, oxygen protection, flow control, vaporiser, circle, ventilator and scavenging.
03Check gas supplies, cylinders, pin indexing, back-up oxygen, power and the theatre, and work safely with oxygen, nitrous oxide, high pressure and anaesthetic agents.
04Run and interpret the pre-use checkout, and do high- and low-pressure and breathing system leak tests by hand, section by section.
05Carry out the manufacturer's PM kit: seals, vaporiser port o-rings, flow sensors, O2 cell, batteries, absorber, valves, bellows or piston.
06Verify flows, ventilator delivery, oxygen failure protection, the hypoxic guard, vaporiser output and the gas module with calibrated analysers, then check scavenging and electrical safety and write a report with measured values.

Curriculum

01What happens to the patient under anaesthesia
Understand what the anaesthesia workstation does for a patient during induction, maintenance and low-flow anaesthesia, and why every design decision in the machine starts from one rule: the patient must always receive oxygen.free
20 min02The whole gas path, from wall outlet to scavenging
Follow a molecule of gas through the workstation, from the pipeline outlet or cylinder to the scavenging system, and name for every stage what it does, how it fails and which test proves it works.22 min03Gas supply, cylinders and the theatre around the machine
Check everything the workstation depends on from outside: pipeline connectors and hoses, pin-indexed cylinders and the back-up oxygen supply, scavenging, electrical supply and the theatre environment, and separate a supply problem from a machine fault.22 min04Safety: fire, gas identity, pressure, vaporisers and waste gas
Work on an anaesthesia workstation without creating a fire risk, a wrong-gas or hypoxic-mixture risk, a high-pressure injury, a vaporiser hazard or an exposure to waste anaesthetic gas, for yourself or for the next patient.22 min05The pre-use checkout: what the morning test proves and what it does not
Know what an automated anaesthesia workstation checkout tests - high- and low-pressure leaks, breathing system leak and compliance, sensors and supplies - what it cannot see, and how to run the same checks by hand on a mechanical machine section by section.25 min06Planned maintenance by the manufacturer's kit
Plan and carry out an anaesthesia workstation PM: identify the kit and intervals for the model, replace seals, vaporiser port o-rings, flow sensors, the O2 cell and batteries, inspect the absorber, valves, bellows or piston, and leave the machine ready for checkout and verification.25 min07Verification with analysers: flows, ventilator, oxygen protection, vaporiser and gas module
Verify an anaesthesia workstation with independent calibrated instruments - fresh gas flow accuracy, ventilator volume, pressure and PEEP, O2 failure protection and the hypoxic guard, vaporiser output with an agent analyser, and the gas module with certified calibration gas - and judge each result only against the manufacturer's limits.25 min08Scavenging, battery, electrical safety and the service report
Check the anaesthetic gas scavenging interface, the backup battery and electrical safety per IEC 62353, and write a service report with measured values that proves the workstation is fit to return to theatre.25 min—Exam
