Ventilator Service — Level 1
Safety, checks and preventive maintenance

The self-test checks the machine against itself. Only an independent gas-flow analyzer on a test lung tells you what the patient would actually receive. This course teaches the engineer who maintains ICU ventilators to keep them safe: gas supply, pneumatics, the PM kit, and the verification that must follow every repair.

Enrol   Try lesson 1 free

A ventilator that passes its self-test can still deliver the wrong breath.

Why this course

  • Ventilators are life support: a wrong volume, a stuck valve or a missed alarm reaches the patient directly.
  • Thousands of donated and second-hand ventilators sit idle because nobody locally is trained to maintain and verify them.
  • Manufacturer courses are expensive and model-specific; the basics every ventilator shares are rarely taught on their own.

After the course you can

Understand what the clinician sets and sees — modes, PEEP, FiO2, triggering — well enough to turn a complaint into a technical question.
Trace the gas path from the wall outlet to the patient and back, and know what each valve and sensor does.
Check gas supply quality and the room: hoses, cylinders, water and oil in medical air, power.
Work safely with oxygen and high-pressure gas and with contaminated parts; never service on a patient.
Run the self-test and know its blind spots; carry out the manufacturer PM kit by hours and by time.
Verify performance with a gas-flow analyzer and a test lung, test every alarm, and write a report with measured values.

Curriculum

01Why a patient needs a ventilator and what the clinician sees
Translate a clinician's complaint about a ventilator - wrong volume, PEEP not held, oxygen alarm, 'it breathes by itself' - into a technical question about a specific part of the machine, its settings or its accessories.free 20 min02The pneumatic chain: from the gas inlet to the patient and back
Trace the gas path of any ICU ventilator from the inlets through regulators, mixing, inspiratory valve, sensors, patient circuit and expiratory valve to the exhaust, and name what each block does and how it typically fails.🔒 22 min03Gas supply and the room
Check, before blaming the ventilator, that the wall outlets, hoses, backup cylinders, medical air quality and electrical supply at the bedspace meet what the ventilator's documentation and the national standard require.🔒 20 min04Safety of the engineer and the patient
Prepare a ventilator for service so that nobody is put at risk: the patient is off the machine, the gas is isolated and depressurised, the contaminated parts are handled correctly, nothing that can feed an oxygen fire touches the gas path, and the machine is tagged until it has been verified.🔒 20 min05Self-test and pre-use check
Run a ventilator's self-test and pre-use check correctly, read a failed step as a pointer to a subsystem, and explain to a clinician or manager why a passed self-test does not replace verification with an independent gas-flow analyzer.🔒 20 min06Preventive maintenance with the manufacturer's kit
Plan preventive maintenance for a ventilator fleet by operating hours and by time, carry out a manufacturer PM kit in the right order, and record it so that the next engineer and an auditor can see exactly what was done.🔒 22 min07Performance verification with a gas-flow analyzer
Set up a calibrated gas-flow analyzer and test lung, verify volume, pressure, PEEP, FiO2 and flow in several modes, test every alarm, the safety valve and battery operation, run IEC 62353 electrical safety, and decide pass or fail strictly against the manufacturer's limits.🔒 25 min08Documentation; transport and non-invasive ventilators in brief
Write a PM report that proves the ventilator is safe, with measured values, instruments and limits, and adapt PM and verification to transport and non-invasive ventilators: batteries, intentional mask leak and oxygen from cylinders.🔒 22 min

FAQ

Is this manufacturer training?

No. It is independent, vendor-neutral training. It does not replace the manufacturer's service procedures, does not grant authorisation or access to service software, and every repair must still be followed by the manufacturer's checkout and performance verification.

Which ventilators does it cover?

The principles shared by ICU ventilators, with examples from common platforms. Model-specific pages are available separately.

What does the certificate say?

Your name, the course, the hours, the date and your exam score, with a unique number and a public verification page.

What do I need?

Basic electrical and pneumatic knowledge. No prior ventilator experience is required for Level 1.

Can my company buy seats for a team?

Yes: five seats and a progress overview for the manager.
