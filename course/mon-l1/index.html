Patient Monitor Service — Level 1

Parameters, simulators and preventive maintenance

ECG, SpO2, NIBP, IBP, temperature, CO2 — each parameter fails in its own way, and most failures are accessories. This course teaches the engineer to test every parameter with a simulator, separate accessory faults from module faults and maintain monitors to a standard that survives an audit.

01

Half of all monitor faults are a cable.

  • A monitor is the only thing watching the patient: a dead SpO2 sensor, a quiet alarm or a monitor missing from the central station goes unnoticed until something happens.
  • Most monitor faults are accessories - cables, sensors, cuffs, sample lines - yet whole monitors are swapped or sent away because nobody separated the accessory from the module.
  • PM is often a sticker and 'checked, OK': no simulator values, no manometer, no leakage on each applied part, and nothing that survives an audit or an incident review.
02

After the course you can

01Understand how each parameter is measured - ECG, oscillometric NIBP, SpO2, IBP, temperature, capnography - well enough to know what can go wrong in each chain.
02Know the monitor's architecture and accessories: modules, cables, sensors, compatibility between technologies, the central station and the room.
03Protect the patient: applied parts BF and CF, defibrillation protection, leakage paths through damaged or wrong accessories, safe cleaning.
04Run a planned maintenance in a fixed order: inspection, accessories, every alarm and its volume, battery, logs, clock and network - and restore the hospital's configuration.
05Verify every channel with a patient simulator, NIBP with a calibrated manometer (static pressure, leak, over-pressure) and capnography with certified test gas.
06Measure leakage on every applied part per IEC 62353, decide pass or fail on values, and write a report with instruments, values and limits.
